Design direction 4Color Block

A friendly product dashboard: warm off-white canvas, big rounded cards, chunky rounded numerals and pill chips, tied together by a bold red/yellow/green band. Decorative colour is always the full triad in signal order (top band, card edges, logo, loader) and never carries state. A single red, yellow or green on its own is always semantic: a lamp, a filled chip or a tinted tile that also says its state in words. Lamps keep red-top order for colour-blind safety.

Colour rules

Decorative vs semantic

Decorative: always all three, always R → Y → G

The band, card edges, logo and loader. Never one colour alone, never tied to data, turns gray when data goes stale.

Semantic: one colour, always with a word

Aspects, levels, flood and link states. FAULT is a separate state, never L4.

How STALE reads

Last values kept, visibly frozen

  • Header link chip flips to Controller LINK LOST, the only red in the header.
  • Every controller-fed card carries an ink STALE — last seen 14:32:40 chip that stays full contrast.
  • Card data drops to grayscale at low opacity under a faint hatch, and the tri-colour band and card edges go gray, so the screen stops looking "live".
  • The countdown is hidden, replaced by a dashed "Countdown hidden" tile. Lamps stop glowing and are labelled Last known.
  • The breach card stays live because breach records arrive from the vision node, which is still ONLINE.
